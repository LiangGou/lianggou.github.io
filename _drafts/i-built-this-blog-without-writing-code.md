## The challenge I set myself

A few days ago I decided to build this blog — and I set one rule: I would not write a single line of code, and I would not touch my laptop. Everything would be done through Meta Muse, from my phone.

You might think building a personal website is trivial — just static HTML, right? Sure, if that's all you want. I wanted more.

## It didn't stay a static page for long

What started as plain HTML grew quickly: a real backend, a polished design, an agent-aware contact system that keeps bots from scraping my information and my email, visitor counters, a newsletter signup, spam prevention. The project touched GitHub, Cloudflare Workers and D1, Formspree, OpenAI's API through an AI gateway, reCAPTCHA — a whole stack I never had to open or sign into myself. Muse structured the codebase, set up the workers, wired the endpoints, configured the bot defenses. For the frontend, I just described what I wanted and reacted to what came back.

## The productivity unlock

The whole exercise genuinely increased my productivity. What would have taken me weekends of context-switching — reading docs, debugging configs, wiring services together — happened in conversation, between other things, from my phone.

## Then I taught it to distribute for me

Once the blog was live, I asked Muse to build me a workflow: every time I publish a post, it drafts a sharp summary with a catchy hook and reshares it to LinkedIn — driving readers back to my own site. LinkedIn is just a distribution channel, a way to attract and engage an audience. My website is the home.

That's the part that still amazes me: I can just ask for a skill, a workflow, and it exists. Next I want to experiment with doing the same for Xiaohongshu — we'll see how far this goes.

## I just talk now

This changed the writing itself, too. Drafting a post used to take real effort — writing it down, polishing it, checking the grammar. I always felt awkward about mistakes. That hurdle is just gone now.

What I do is simple: I turn on the mic and talk. Continuously, following my train of thought, dumping every idea in my head. My thinking wanders and doubles back — that's fine. Once it's transcribed, the AI organizes it. Then I look at the structure, move sections around, make the main theme stand out. It's usually already pretty good; I just do minor touch-ups.

The amazing part: Muse has picked up my style — how I talk, how I interact with it. The writing still has a slight AI flavor, but it carries my voice.

## The honest caveat

Here's the interesting part: I think my technical background made this much easier. I knew what to ask for. I could smell when something was off. I recommended Muse to my wife — she's a math teacher — and her experience was different. She tried it for updating slides and preparing teaching materials, and found ChatGPT better at explaining math and handling PowerPoint. Fair enough.

My working theory now: these tools serve different jobs. Muse is a great personal assistant — errands, small projects, getting things built. GPT is strong at math and general conversation. Anthropic's models shine at coding and workplace tasks. Different tools, different purposes. The trick is knowing which job you actually have.
